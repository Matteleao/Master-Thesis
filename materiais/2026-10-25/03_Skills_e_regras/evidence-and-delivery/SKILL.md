# Evidence and delivery

## Honesty rules

Never invent a number, a citation, a source, a year, a finding or a precision. Verify claims against the actual source or the actual thesis document, never against drafts previously produced in a working folder.

When a claim cannot be sourced, say so explicitly and offer the options: provide a source, weaken the claim, or remove it. Mark uncertain transcriptions and missing citation years visibly instead of filling them.

Distinguish three things that are easy to blur: what the work demonstrated, what it was designed to enable, and what is only an expected benefit.

Limitations must name which inference was weakened, not merely state that limitations exist. Locate uncertainty in a concrete condition, sample or measurement.

Never present a rule as coming from a source that does not contain it.

**A worked case.** An earlier draft carried "the median investigation was conducted at four sites with a median target enrollment of 80 participants". Those figures did not appear in any file under review. The correct move was to remove them, state the volume constraint without a figure, and say that the recalculation had not been done. Not to find a plausible substitute.

This rule also bounds the predictable-vocabulary and sentence-variation work in scientific-prose. Making a claim more specific is good practice only when the added specificity is verified. A number, name, or date inserted only to make a sentence sound less generic, with no source behind it, is fabrication wearing the shape of a stylistic fix, not a style improvement.

## Verb strength

Match the verb to the strength of the evidence.

| Verb | Requires |
| :-- | :-- |
| demonstrates | strong evidence and an adequate design |
| indicates, suggests | supported interpretation |
| is consistent with | a result that merely fails to contradict |
| is associated with | correlation, never implying cause |

These verbs survive the plainness rule in the scientific-prose skill, because the choice between them carries the evidential claim.

Always separate the result from its interpretation. Result: *We found that X increased Y.* Interpretation: *Our results suggest that X increases Y.*

## Information density

The strongest marker of artificial text is high fluency with low verifiable content. Every central claim must carry something observable, measurable or methodologically justifiable.

Where a work has no experimental data, density comes from elsewhere: clause numbers and named instruments instead of *the regulation requires*; published magnitudes such as kappa values, case counts, percentages and years; the decision explained together with the alternative rejected and the reason; and the exact boundary at which a claim stops holding.

## Terminology and abbreviations

One object, one name. Never vary the term for elegance. Repeating the correct technical term beats synonym drift. This rule overrides the plain-vocabulary preference: a defined term stays exact even when a plainer word exists.

Define an abbreviation at first mention, then use it consistently and never revert to the full form. Verify that the definition precedes every use, including in text inserted later. Keep the set small and do not abbreviate a term used only once. Standard SI units need no definition. Define statistical abbreviations in a thesis. Full names of documents and formal legal citations keep their full form even after the abbreviation exists.

## Voice, tense and numbers

Check that voice does not alternate for the same actor, and that tense matches scientific function in each passage: simple present for established knowledge, interpretation and definitions; simple past for what this work did and found.

Numbers must be traceable to a source or a calculation. Check that every cross-reference points at an element that exists under the current numbering, which matters most after renumbering tables or merging sections.

## Rule precedence

1. Requirements of the university, programme, journal or funding body
2. The selected style manual
3. These skills
4. Where two valid options remain, pick one and hold it consistently across the whole document

## Before delivering

Run these as checks. Do not claim the text is clean without measuring.

**Cohesion first**, because the other checks are prohibitions and a text audited only against prohibitions comes out chopped.

1. Find every run of three or more consecutive sentences that each open a fresh subject with no connective, and bind the ones where a chain of consequence runs through them. Check that each chain closes on a sentence marked as the consequence

**Structure and register next.** The argument-structure and scientific-prose skills hold the substance of items 2 to 8.

2. Count opposition constructions, one-sentence and two-sentence forms. State which genuine distinction each survivor carries
3. Map each section's development pattern in one phrase. Two adjacent sections sharing a pattern means one gets rewritten
4. Count short declarative sentences under eight words. Bind first, then apply the deletion test to whatever is left, and check that none sit next to each other
5. List every absolute quantifier and scope it to what was examined
6. Find every coordinated series of three, including ones whose three items are real. Convert all but one or two, and bind the result where the conversion produced separate sentences. Do the same for series of four and for runs of parallel clauses
7. Scan every verb and framing noun for a plainer equivalent, including words on no list
8. Read three consecutive paragraphs aloud. An audible cadence means the structure is repeating. A reading that keeps stopping and restarting means the sentences were chopped

**Sentence level, with target counts.**

9. `rather than`, `and not`, `instead of`, `not X but Y` — reduce to zero
10. Self-commenting tails. Search `, and it `, `, and this `, `, and that `, `, which is the `. The search returns candidates, never verdicts: keep every second clause that carries new content, and delete only tails that comment on the status of the clause before them
11. First sentence of every paragraph — no announcing opener
12. Em dashes, en dashes, exclamation marks, contractions — all zero
13. British spellings against the American convention, allowing proper nouns — zero
14. Spread of sentence and paragraph lengths, not a mean. If sentences all come out the same size, the text was written to a shape. Check separately that no long sentence has to be read twice and no short ones are stacked
15. Clefts, fronted participials, pied-piped relatives. `Taken together` is the one that recurs
16. Meta-commentary about the writing, and any sentence built to be quotable

**Content.**

17. Each abbreviation defined before first use, never alternating with the full form
18. Voice does not alternate for the same actor
19. Tense matches scientific function in each passage
20. Every evaluative adjective has a criterion, and empty intensifiers are replaced by the figure
21. Terminology has not drifted across synonyms
22. Numbers follow the numeral rules and are traceable
23. Every cross-reference points at an element that exists under the current numbering
24. Each paragraph has one function and does not restate another
25. Scan for the flagged predictable-vocabulary words that survived the plain-word substitution (scientific-prose), and for the same discourse marker opening two paragraphs in one section

## Document conventions for deliverables

When producing a thesis chapter as a file:

| Item | Decision |
| :-- | :-- |
| Alignment and layout | Justified, first-line indent 720 twips, no blank line between paragraphs |
| Headings | Word Heading 1 and Heading 2 styles, so the navigation pane builds |
| Format | The author prefers downloadable deliverables, .docx or .html, over inline code blocks |

When a table is recovered from an older version of the thesis, convert its spelling, terminology and cross-references to the current version before inserting it, and state what was changed and what could not be verified.

## How to report

Reply to the author in Portuguese. Deliver academic text in English.

Be direct, disagree when there is a reason, and say clearly when something cannot be verified. When he supplies his own well-written sentence that breaks a rule, say so and offer the trade-off; consistency outranks elegance in his stated preference.

Discuss and close the plan before generating files. Do not rewrite an approved plan without warning; signal structural changes first.

Report changes, unresolved verification issues, and the decisions for the author. Close with a numbered action summary and sequenced questions. Do not claim that a text is clean merely because the scan was performed.
