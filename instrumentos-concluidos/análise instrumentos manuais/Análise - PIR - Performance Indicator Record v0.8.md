# Critical analysis — PIR: Performance Indicator Record v0.8

**Status:** manual analysis to guide the next revision. The PIR HTML was not modified in this step.

## 1. Executive verdict

The PIR v0.8 has the right overall purpose and is closer to the thesis than the earlier oversized redesigns. It should remain a **periodic, aggregate performance-oversight record** for one study–site–period. It should not become a second FUCR, IMC, CAPA Log, SRR or POT.

The central decision supported by the PIR is:

> **Whether the site’s predefined performance indicators show control, drift, a need for closer review, or a need for correction, CAPA or escalation during a defined review period.**

The v0.8 architecture already contains most of the correct layers:

1. review population and period;
2. indicator catalogue and pre-specified rule configuration;
3. source reconciliation;
4. consolidated results;
5. interpretation;
6. indicator detail;
7. signal, decision and action route;
8. effectiveness and closure.

That architecture should be preserved.

However, the current filled demonstration exposes serious semantic and traceability problems that would be unacceptable in a controlled blank template if they were copied into future versions:

- indicator IDs, names and rules are crossed in the consolidated results;
- some rows use a denominator belonging to another indicator;
- some source keys do not match the result being described;
- the example logic is not consistently carried through from catalogue to reconciliation, result, detail and action;
- the six indicators are not always distinguishable from their source records;
- the safety indicator is confused with protocol-deviation or follow-up terminology in places;
- the PIR sometimes states that a result is “within rule” without showing the rule threshold in the same decision context;
- the action route is useful but needs a stricter distinction between monitoring, correction, CAPA and escalation;
- the overall conclusion can be read as a general quality verdict instead of a bounded decision for the stated period and source boundary.

**Recommendation:** keep the six-/seven-page functional structure only if it remains usable, but rebuild the content conservatively around one continuous example, exact source references, fixed rules and correct indicator ownership. Do not add more indicator families or turn every source instrument into a repeated mini-table.

## 2. What the PIR is and is not

### 2.1. What it is

The PIR is the site’s **periodic performance oversight record**. It takes existing records generated by operational instruments and converts them into a small, predefined set of review-period indicators.

It answers questions such as:

- Is enrollment quality still controlled?
- Are protocol deviations recurring or concentrating in one area?
- Are follow-up visits producing usable data within the expected windows?
- Are reportable safety cases reaching the sponsor within the applicable CIP interval and with complete first reports?
- Is the surgeon’s procedure delivery stable in the current learning-curve context?
- Is critical data being completed, reconciled and finalized on time?

The PIR does not collect the underlying participant, visit, procedure, imaging or safety facts again. It records:

- the rule fixed in advance;
- the population and denominator;
- the exact source boundary;
- the calculated result;
- its interpretation under the rule;
- the decision and route;
- the owner and follow-up;
- the effectiveness evidence.

### 2.2. What it is not

The PIR is not:

- a participant eligibility record;
- a screening log;
- a follow-up visit record;
- a FUCR exception log;
- a safety case record;
- a SART case-tracking record;
- a deviation log;
- an IMC checklist;
- a CAPA case file;
- a site risk register;
- a PI decision record;
- a statistical comparison of surgeons or sites;
- a sponsor quality-tolerance-limit dashboard;
- an authorization or activation record.

A PIR result can trigger or feed those instruments, but the PIR should not reproduce their full content.

## 3. The decision, unit, population and period

### 3.1. Decision sentence

The first page should state the decision in direct language:

> After completing this record, the reviewer should be able to decide whether each predefined site-performance indicator is **within control**, **under closer review**, **requiring action**, or **not assessable**, and whether the resulting signal must be routed to monitoring, correction, CAPA, PI oversight, sponsor clarification or another controlled record.

The overall conclusion must be narrower than “the study is good” or “the site is compliant”. It is a conclusion about the selected processes, sources and review period.

### 3.2. Unit of analysis

The unit should be explicit:

```text
one indicator ID for one study–site–review period, with a defined population or activity base
```

The PIR may contain multiple indicator rows in one review, but every result must retain its own:

- indicator ID;
- rule version;
- numerator or value;
- denominator or activity base;
- source boundary;
- interpretation;
- route.

### 3.3. Population and period

The current v0.8 correctly asks for study, site, period and data cut-off. That information must remain before the indicator results.

The first page should not use a generic “review type” as a substitute for the actual population. It should define:

- study/protocol;
- site;
- review period;
- data cut-off;
- review trigger;
- accountable reviewer;
- whether the review is routine, early-case, amendment-triggered, trend-triggered, CAPA-effectiveness, or another predefined type.

The population must be expressed in the units that the indicator actually uses:

- participant decisions for enrollment quality;
- procedures, expected visits, eligibility decisions, documentation events or another activity-specific base for protocol deviations;
- expected visits and due participants for follow-up compliance;
- reportable events for safety reporting timeliness;
- comparable procedures per surgeon for procedural consistency;
- endpoint-critical fields, queries, operative notes and sampled source–CRF entries for critical data completeness.

The PIR must never force one denominator across all indicators.

## 4. What the thesis requires

The thesis defines Performance Oversight as a small set of indicators designed to identify loss of control while correction is still possible. The six indicators answer DR8 and support the proactive corrective/preventive cycle required by DR5.

The thesis makes five design points that must govern the PIR.

### 4.1. Six indicators, not a general dashboard

The selected indicators are:

1. **Enrollment quality**;
2. **Protocol deviation rate**;
3. **Follow-up compliance**;
4. **Safety reporting timeliness**;
5. **Surgeon procedural consistency**;
6. **Critical data completeness**.

They cover different CtQ domains and different types of activity. The PIR should not merge them into a single score.

### 4.2. Rules are fixed before results are known

Before the first participant is enrolled, the site fixes:

- the definition;
- the scope boundary;
- the source records;
- the numerator and denominator;
- the review cadence;
- the thresholds or decision conditions;
- the closer-review condition;
- the action condition;
- the route when action is required.

The rule must not be changed retrospectively to fit the observed result. If a rule is amended prospectively, the PIR must record the new version and the effective date; it must not silently reinterpret the prior period.

### 4.3. Small denominators require counts and context

The thesis explicitly warns that a single site often has few cases. The PIR should therefore record counts with denominators first. Percentages can be added when they remain interpretable.

A percentage alone is unsafe when:

- the denominator is small;
- the underlying events have different materiality;
- one important event is hidden by many minor events;
- different units are combined;
- cases are clustered by surgeon, assessor or time point.

### 4.4. The PIR reuses existing records

The PIR should not add a new data-collection burden. Its inputs are existing records from the framework and, where applicable, controlled clinical or sponsor records.

The form therefore needs strong source reconciliation, not a second narrative of every event.

### 4.5. A result must lead somewhere

A negative or uncertain result must have a destination:

- closer review;
- immediate correction;
- FUCR follow-up;
- IMC verification;
- CAPA investigation;
- SRR risk update;
- POT/PI decision;
- sponsor clarification;
- safety route;
- credentialing or proctoring route;
- limitation documented because the result cannot yet be assessed.

“Action required” without a named owner, receiving record and effectiveness check is not a complete PIR output.

## 5. Assessment of the current architecture

### 5.1. Purpose and Use

The current Purpose and Use table is appropriate. It correctly states:

- the unit as study–site–period and indicator ID;
- the decision as stable, drifting or requiring action;
- inputs as existing source records, rules, denominators and trends;
- outputs as reproducible results, interpretation, action route and effectiveness record.

The wording should be slightly tightened so that “stable” does not sound like a statistical claim. Prefer:

```text
within predefined control rule; closer review; action required; not assessable
```

The table should also say that a result is bounded by the stated source boundary and cut-off.

### 5.2. Review Population and Period

This section belongs before the catalogue and results. It is one of the strongest parts of the form because it prevents a result from floating free of its population.

It should retain:

- study/site/review period;
- cut-off;
- exact source boundary;
- excluded records or activities;
- trigger and reason;
- accountable reviewer.

It should add a field for:

- **rule-set version in force for this review**;
- **whether any rule or source changed during the period**;
- **impact of the change on comparability**.

If an amendment took effect during the period, the reviewer should either split the period or show the two applicable rule versions. A single result that silently spans two rules is not auditable.

### 5.3. Indicator Catalogue and Control Configuration

This table is conceptually necessary and should not be removed. It is not a result table; it is the pre-specified configuration.

Each indicator row should include:

- indicator ID;
- indicator name;
- what it measures;
- CtQ/domain or decision purpose;
- source group;
- population unit;
- cadence;
- rule version;
- threshold or decision condition;
- scope boundary;
- accountable reviewer;
- receiving route if a signal occurs.

The current table is too compressed in the rule/scope area. The phrase “Rule / trigger version” is not enough if it does not distinguish:

- rule identifier;
- rule text or short operational summary;
- version;
- effective date.

The configuration must be defined before the result. The blank version should leave the result cells empty while retaining a clear first example row in the agreed light-blue style, if examples are desired.

### 5.4. Source Reconciliation and Indicator Basis

This is one of the most important PIR sections. It should remain.

The correct logic is:

```text
source group → exact records reviewed → population/activity base → calculation basis → reconciliation result → rule reference
```

The current v0.8 improves over earlier drafts because it asks for exact record codes and does not merely say “PFVR/FUCR aggregate”. That is correct.

However, the section must prohibit a common error: repeating the source group in “Indicator basis”. The basis must state what is counted and against what.

For example:

```text
Source group: PFVR + FUCR
Indicator basis: visits completed within window / expected visits; complete visits / completed visits; endpoint-relevant missing components / visits requiring the component; participants not retrieved / participants due
```

It should not say merely “follow-up compliance”.

The reconciliation checks should explicitly identify:

- duplicate records;
- records outside the period;
- records created after cut-off;
- excluded records;
- missing source records;
- source-to-source mismatch;
- denominator change caused by the mismatch;
- open query or unresolved limitation.

A “Complete” reconciliation result is inappropriate if an unresolved mismatch could change the numerator or denominator. In that case, the result should be “Complete with limitation” or “Not assessable”, with impact stated.

### 5.5. Consolidated Results

A consolidated table is useful, but only if each row is internally coherent. The current filled demo shows the central problem: indicator labels, results, rules and source keys are not reliably aligned.

The result table should use a fixed row schema:

```text
Indicator ID | Numerator/value | Denominator/activity base | Result | Rule comparison | Decision | Exact source key
```

Every row should answer one indicator only. The reviewer should not be forced to infer whether a value belongs to enrollment quality, protocol deviation rate, follow-up compliance or safety reporting.

The “Rule comparison” cell should say one of:

- within control rule;
- closer review threshold reached;
- action threshold reached;
- not assessable because…

It should not merely say “improved” or “monitor”. A trend is not the same as a rule comparison.

### 5.6. Indicator Interpretation

This section is valuable and should remain, but it must be tied to the result row.

The interpretation should answer three questions:

1. What does the result support the site deciding now?
2. What does it not establish?
3. Where does the decision go next?

The current wording generally follows this logic and is one of the better design choices in v0.8. It prevents the PIR from overstating what a small site-level result can prove.

The source key must refer to the exact records that support the interpretation. A generic source group is insufficient.

### 5.7. Indicator Detail

A detail section is needed because six aggregate rows alone cannot show the materiality, recurrence or concentration behind a signal. The current detail tables are directionally correct.

However, the detail section should not become a second full database. It should include only the measures that materially change the indicator decision.

The recommended detail logic is one unified section with repeated indicator blocks, not unrelated mini-forms:

- Enrollment quality;
- Protocol deviation profile;
- Follow-up compliance;
- Safety reporting timeliness;
- Surgeon procedural consistency;
- Critical data completeness.

Each block should retain the same compact structure:

```text
measure | numerator/value | denominator/base | result | pattern/limitation | linked record
```

The exact linked record is essential. For example, “PSEAR-021-017; PSEAR-021-022 §D–F” is useful; “PSEAR review” is not.

The current demonstration’s use of “ICQL” in an imaging-related deviation line should be checked against the actual instrument name and source record. The PIR must use the canonical instrument acronym consistently.

### 5.8. Signal, Decision and Action Route

This section is a strong concept and should remain. It creates the bridge from measurement to action.

The route should distinguish:

- **monitoring:** no immediate correction; review again at a defined next period;
- **correction:** a specific isolated error is corrected and verified;
- **closer review:** additional review or sampling is planned;
- **CAPA:** recurrence, systemic cause or trend requires formal corrective/preventive investigation;
- **escalation:** PI, sponsor, credentialing or safety route must decide;
- **not assessable:** evidence is insufficient and a recovery action is needed.

The current action table has the right fields:

- finding;
- route;
- receiving record and section;
- impact/materiality;
- named owner;
- due date;
- status.

The owner must be a named person, not only a generic “lead”. The receiving route must reference a real record ID and section.

### 5.9. Action closure and effectiveness

The effectiveness table is necessary. The PIR should not close an action merely because someone performed a correction. It should state the criterion that will show whether the process returned to control.

The current fields are appropriate:

- action or signal;
- action type;
- effectiveness evidence/receiving record;
- effectiveness criterion;
- check due;
- status.

The blank template should make clear that effectiveness can be:

- pending;
- demonstrated;
- not demonstrated;
- not yet assessable with documented limitation.

If the indicator returns under control, the PIR can provide the effectiveness evidence. If not, the case should continue into CAPA, SRR, POT or the relevant escalation route.

### 5.10. Overall conclusion and review

The overall conclusion should not be a free-form generic paragraph only. It should summarize:

- indicators within control;
- indicators under closer review;
- indicators requiring action;
- indicators not assessable;
- open actions;
- whether CAPA or escalation was required;
- limitations that qualify the conclusion.

A useful overall conclusion is bounded:

```text
For the defined study–site–Q3 2026 period and the source records listed in Section B, four indicators were within their predefined rules, one required closer review, and one was not assessable because [...]. No conclusion is made about periods or source records outside this boundary.
```

The conclusion must not state “the site is compliant” without qualification.

## 6. Indicator-by-indicator assessment against the thesis

### 6.1. EQ-01 — Enrollment quality

#### What it measures

Whether each inclusion decision was correct and supported by all critical evidence before the decision.

#### Correct denominator

All inclusion decisions made during the review period. The thesis favors reviewing every inclusion at a single site because sampling would leave too many decisions unreviewed.

#### Required measures

- inclusions with all critical criteria evidenced before decision;
- eligibility errors or clarifications found after enrollment;
- important eligibility deviations;
- recurring ambiguity by criterion;
- concentration by assessor or criterion where relevant.

#### Correct rule logic

- within control: every critical criterion was evidenced before every inclusion decision;
- closer review: evidence was completed after the decision but before surgery, or ambiguity recurs;
- action: eligibility error after enrollment or important eligibility deviation;
- CAPA: uncertainty recurs after clarification/training.

#### Required sources

- PSEAR or the site’s eligibility records;
- SIFSL for population reconciliation and reasons for non-inclusion where applicable;
- deviation records;
- sponsor clarification or amendment record when a criterion is ambiguous.

#### PIR caution

Do not use screening volume as the denominator for enrollment quality. SIFSL records recruitment and non-inclusion; EQ-01 evaluates the quality of inclusion decisions. Those are connected but not identical.

### 6.2. PD-01 — Protocol deviation rate/profile

#### What it measures

Where protocol deviations occur, how material they are, whether they recur and whether they concentrate in an activity, phase, surgeon, assessor or process.

#### Correct denominator

The denominator must follow the activity:

- procedures for procedural deviations;
- expected visits for visit-window deviations;
- participant decisions for eligibility-related deviations;
- relevant records or activities for documentation deviations;
- another explicit base where the deviation type requires it.

There must not be one global deviation percentage combining incompatible units.

#### Required measures

- event count by area;
- activity-specific denominator;
- materiality/importance;
- recurrence;
- concentration;
- linked CAPA or action record;
- source ownership if the sponsor holds the formal deviation record.

#### Correct rule logic

- no important deviation and minor deviations below the advance-set limit: within control;
- several minor deviations in one area or recurrence from the previous period: closer review;
- any important deviation: action;
- important recurrence or defined cluster: root-cause review and possible CAPA or sponsor escalation.

#### PIR caution

The current demo contains a serious semantic mismatch where a row labeled PD-01 is populated with an eligibility-style result. That must be corrected. PD-01 must never use an enrollment-quality numerator/denominator simply because both happen to contain “30”.

### 6.3. FC-01 — Follow-up compliance

#### What it measures

Whether follow-up produces usable data through on-time visits and required assessments.

#### Required four measures

The thesis requires separate counts and denominators:

1. visits completed within the window / visits expected;
2. visits containing every required component / visits completed;
3. visits missing an endpoint-needed component / visits requiring that component;
4. participants whose follow-up could not be obtained despite documented efforts / participants due for follow-up.

Each denominator must be displayed next to its numerator.

#### Required cause classification

The cause should distinguish:

- participant-related;
- site-process-related;
- protocol/design-related;
- other or not assessable.

This distinction prevents every delayed or missed visit from being treated automatically as a site failure.

#### Correct rule logic

- at/above the predefined window threshold and no endpoint component missing: within control;
- intermediate window performance or one missing endpoint component: closer review;
- below the lower threshold, two or more endpoint component omissions, or participant lost despite documented efforts: action;
- recurrence after the initial response: CAPA.

#### Interface

The PFVR is the individual source record. The FUCR performs the period-level follow-up review and assigns causes. The PIR should receive reconciled FUCR results, not re-count every PFVR line unless the source reconciliation specifically requires a verification sample.

#### PIR caution

The current demo uses “86 complete / 86 expected” in a way that may conceal the difference between a visit being complete and a visit being within its window. Those are distinct measures and must not be collapsed.

### 6.4. ST-01 — Safety reporting timeliness

#### What it measures

Whether each reportable event covered by the CIP reaches the sponsor within the applicable interval, with a complete first report and timely follow-up information.

#### Scope

The thesis limits the indicator to the reportable scope defined by the CIP, including SAEs and device deficiencies that could have led to a SADE where applicable. The sponsor’s regulatory reports remain outside the site indicator’s scope.

Because the safety taxonomy is being updated for ISO 14155:2026 and the SART is being reconceived, the PIR must not hard-code obsolete terminology. It should reference the current SART classification and the current CIP.

#### Required measures

- initial notifications timely / reportable events;
- complete first reports / reportable events;
- timely follow-up information / events requiring follow-up;
- cases still open beyond the permitted interval;
- stage where delay arose;
- linked deviation or action where applicable.

#### Correct rule logic

- every initial notification timely and every first report complete: within control;
- corrected first report or late follow-up: closer review;
- late initial notification or case open beyond the applicable interval: action and PI referral;
- recurring delay at the same stage: process change and possible CAPA.

#### PIR caution

The current demo has a row labeled “ST-01” under a follow-up-style result and elsewhere uses safety terminology in a way that needs systematic normalization. Every safety result must be traceable to the SART event/case IDs and the CIP interval in force.

### 6.5. SP-01 — Surgeon procedural consistency

#### What it measures

Whether delivery of the investigational procedure is stable and aligned with the CIP for each surgeon and review period, in the context of the learning curve.

#### Required measures

- unplanned component substitutions;
- procedural deviations;
- operative duration for comparable cases;
- senior support/proctoring;
- implant configuration and position documentation;
- early technical complications already reviewed through the complication pathway.

#### Correct interpretation

This is not a surgeon ranking or statistical performance comparison. The site should interpret the signals against:

- the surgeon’s own earlier cases;
- comparable complexity;
- the learning-curve phase;
- clinical and technical context;
- clustering and case mix limitations.

#### Correct rule logic

- support followed the plan and any substitution/deviation was isolated with a non-technical cause: within control;
- unclear cause, unexpected duration or documentation gap: closer review;
- same signal twice in one period, suspected device/technique cause of early complication, or repeated duration issue: action and review of the learning curve;
- possible authorization change: return to credentialing/POT decision.

#### Interface

SLCT, ICDL and CSST provide the underlying information. The PIR summarizes it for the period. It does not replace the clinical review or change authorization by itself.

#### PIR caution

Avoid presenting “no concentration” as proof of procedural safety. It is an interpretation bounded by small numbers and case-mix limitations.

### 6.6. DC-01 — Critical data completeness

#### What it measures

Whether endpoint- and safety-critical data are available, current and source-consistent during conduct.

#### Required measures

The thesis identifies four measures:

1. endpoint-critical fields completed within the interval for each visit type;
2. open queries and median age;
3. operative notes finalized on time;
4. sampled CRF entries agreeing with source when the data were first recorded outside the CRF.

The fourth measure must not be omitted simply because it comes from an IMC or source-verification cycle.

#### Correct rule logic

- all measures within their predefined values and every sampled entry agrees with source: within control;
- one measure outside its value: closer review;
- shortfall beyond the lower value, repeated late notes or unresolved source–CRF discrepancy: action;
- deterioration of two measures across two consecutive periods or recurrence after initial response: CAPA.

#### PIR caution

Open query count and median age need a clear base. “3 open queries / 3 open queries” is not automatically meaningful. The form should record:

- current open queries;
- query population or review boundary;
- median age calculation basis;
- critical versus non-critical status;
- whether queries can change the reported result.

## 7. The most important errors visible in the current filled demo

The following are not minor cosmetic concerns. They must be corrected before a blank version is treated as a controlled template.

### 7.1. Indicator/result mismatch

The consolidated results show examples in which the indicator ID, description, result and source key do not represent the same phenomenon. A result about eligibility quality must not appear under PD-01. A result about follow-up timing must not be labeled as a safety indicator.

### 7.2. Rule/result mismatch

Some results are compared with the wrong rule version, for example a follow-up-style row being described as within a protocol-deviation rule. The rule ID must be carried from the catalogue through reconciliation and result.

### 7.3. Source-key mismatch

The source key must be the exact record boundary for that result. If the row says protocol deviation rate, the source key should identify the deviation records and activity base. It should not point to PSEAR merely because PSEAR appears elsewhere in the PIR.

### 7.4. Denominator mismatch

The same number can be a participant count, procedure count or visit count. The form must make the unit impossible to confuse. The denominator must appear in the result row and in the detail block.

### 7.5. Source reconciliation is not yet a guarantee of calculation validity

A source set can be complete and still be the wrong source set for the indicator. The reconciliation section must verify both:

- all records in the stated boundary are present;
- those records are appropriate for the indicator basis.

### 7.6. “Complete” is overused

Complete should mean that the source reconciliation and calculation basis are adequate for the stated result. If a result is limited by an open query or a missing source record, the status should carry that limitation.

### 7.7. The filled example must use one continuous story

The example is valuable only when a reviewer can follow one indicator from:

```text
catalogue → source reconciliation → consolidated result → detail → interpretation → action → effectiveness
```

The current demo partially does this but breaks continuity where IDs and source keys change. The next example should follow one consistent Q3 scenario across all sections.

## 8. Recommended future structure without expanding the instrument unnecessarily

The PIR should remain a compact periodic record. The following sequence is recommended.

### Section A — Purpose and Use

Include the fixed four-cell table:

- Unit of analysis;
- Decision support;
- Inputs;
- Outputs.

State clearly that the PIR is aggregate and does not replace source instruments.

### Section B — Review Population, Period and Scope

Fields:

- study/protocol;
- site;
- period;
- cut-off;
- trigger/reason;
- population/activity bases;
- exclusions and limitations;
- accountable reviewer;
- applicable rule-set version.

### Section C — Indicator Catalogue and Pre-specified Rules

One row per indicator:

- ID;
- name;
- purpose/CtQ;
- source group;
- unit;
- cadence;
- rule/version/effective date;
- scope boundary;
- receiving route;
- accountable reviewer.

No observed results in this section.

### Section D — Source Reconciliation and Indicator Basis

One row per indicator or source group, depending on the design:

- indicator ID;
- exact record codes/ranges;
- population/activity base;
- numerator/denominator basis;
- exclusions;
- reconciliation result;
- limitation;
- rule source/version.

The basis must not repeat the source-group name.

### Section E — Consolidated Results

One row per indicator:

- ID;
- numerator/value;
- denominator/base;
- result;
- rule comparison;
- decision;
- exact source key.

### Section F — Interpretation

One row per indicator:

- observed result/limitation;
- decision supported now;
- what it does not establish;
- receiving route;
- linked record.

### Section G — Indicator Detail

One combined section with one compact block per indicator. Do not create unnecessary separate sections if the same table schema can be reused.

### Section H — Signal, Decision and Action Route

Only findings that require a decision. Fields:

- finding/signal;
- decision/route;
- receiving record and section;
- materiality/impact;
- named owner;
- due date;
- status.

### Section I — Effectiveness and Closure

Fields:

- action/signal;
- action type;
- effectiveness evidence;
- effectiveness criterion;
- check date;
- result;
- closure/continued escalation.

### Section J — Overall Conclusion and Certification

Summarize the period-bound conclusion and limitations. Include named reviewer, role, signature/date and PI/accountable review where required by the risk level.

This structure is essentially the current v0.8 logic, corrected for identity and sequence. It does not require adding another dashboard or a new data collection section.

## 9. Integration with the other instruments

### 9.1. PSEAR/SIFSL → EQ-01

- PSEAR supplies individual eligibility decisions and evidence status.
- SIFSL supplies the population of candidates, screening outcomes and reconciliation of inclusions/non-inclusions.
- Deviation records supply important eligibility deviations.
- PIR calculates enrollment-quality performance and routes ambiguity, error or recurrence.

The PIR must not reproduce the full PSEAR. It needs exact record references and aggregate counts.

### 9.2. FUCR/PFVR → FC-01

- PFVR records one participant’s visit and components.
- FUCR aggregates the review-period follow-up performance and assigns causes.
- PIR uses the FUCR’s reconciled period result and, when necessary, verifies the source boundary.

The PIR must not become a second FUCR. It should report the four required follow-up measures and the decision under the predefined rule.

### 9.3. SART → ST-01

- SART records and tracks an individual safety case under the current ISO/CIP terminology.
- PIR measures the timeliness and completeness of the site’s reporting process.
- SCML and CSST review patterns and complication signals, not the PIR’s reporting timeliness calculation.

The PIR must cite SART case IDs and the applicable CIP reporting window. It must not reproduce case narratives.

### 9.4. Deviation records/IMC/CAPA → PD-01

- The deviation record is the event source.
- IMC may identify or verify deviations.
- CAPA handles systemic recurrence or a formal correction/prevention cycle.
- PIR summarizes frequency, materiality, recurrence and concentration by activity-specific denominator.

The PIR should not create a deviation record for every result; it should reference the record that already exists.

### 9.5. ICDL/SLCT/CSST → SP-01

- ICDL provides procedure-level evidence.
- SLCT tracks the surgeon’s case sequence and learning-curve context.
- CSST contributes complication-pattern review where relevant.
- PIR summarizes the period and routes a potential authorization issue to credentialing/POT.

PIR is not a statistical surgeon-ranking tool.

### 9.6. IMC/source records → DC-01

- IMC verifies execution, evidence and source agreement during a defined review.
- Query log, operative notes and critical-field records provide the other measures.
- PIR aggregates them into the data-completeness indicator.

The PIR must make clear whether a result comes from all records or an IMC sample.

### 9.7. PIR → CAPA, SRR, POT and MRL

The PIR output is a performance signal and route, not an automatic decision in another instrument.

- CAPA receives recurrence or systemic failure;
- SRR receives a persistent or consequential site risk;
- POT receives a PI-level oversight decision;
- MRL may receive a milestone/lifecycle review input when the indicator affects a gate or readiness decision;
- FUCR/IMC/SART/SLCT receive operational follow-up actions where those are the appropriate owners.

The PIR should state the receiving record and section rather than merely naming an instrument.

## 10. Rule and threshold design

### 10.1. Use thresholds as decision aids, not false precision

The thesis states that most site-level thresholds are provisional and must be calibrated as experience accumulates. The PIR should therefore identify:

- threshold source;
- version;
- effective date;
- whether it is provisional or established;
- what decision it triggers.

It should not imply that a percentage is a validated universal performance standard.

### 10.2. Separate three levels of interpretation

For each indicator, use three distinct states:

1. **Within control:** the predefined condition is met;
2. **Closer review:** the result is not yet an action failure, but the signal requires additional attention;
3. **Action required:** the predefined action condition is met.

A fourth state is needed:

4. **Not assessable:** the source or denominator is insufficient to support the decision.

### 10.3. Materiality can override a percentage

For protocol deviations and safety reporting, one important event may require action even when the rate is low. The PIR must show materiality separately from frequency.

### 10.4. Trends require comparable periods

A trend comparison should state:

- prior period;
- same rule version or how rule changes were handled;
- same denominator definition;
- whether the population is comparable;
- what changed operationally.

“Improved” alone is not an auditable trend statement.

### 10.5. Concentration is not causation

The PIR may show concentration by surgeon, assessor, visit type, criterion or process. It should not state that concentration proves causation. It should route the signal to the appropriate clinical or process review.

## 13. Main recommendations, in priority order

### Priority 1 — Correct semantic identity

Fix all indicator ID/name/rule/source mismatches. This is mandatory before visual refinement.

### Priority 2 — Enforce denominator discipline

Display the denominator or activity base beside every result. Do not combine participants, visits, procedures, events and fields.

### Priority 3 — Carry a stable indicator ID through the entire form

The same ID must appear in catalogue, reconciliation, result, detail, interpretation and action.

### Priority 4 — Make rule comparison explicit

Every result must state whether it is within control, closer review, action required or not assessable under the rule version in force.

### Priority 5 — Preserve exact source references

Use record codes/ranges and sections. “PSEAR”, “FUCR” or “SART” alone is not sufficient.

### Priority 6 — Preserve the distinction between PIR and sibling instruments

Do not turn the PIR into a second FUCR, IMC, CAPA or source record.

### Priority 7 — Make action routes operational

Every action needs a named owner, due date, receiving record/section, status and effectiveness criterion.

### Priority 8 — Bound the overall conclusion

State the study, site, period, cut-off and source limitation in the conclusion.

### Priority 9 — Normalize safety terminology after SART revision

The PIR should reference the current SART and CIP classification without hard-coding terminology that the ISO 14155:2026 update has superseded.

### Priority 10 — Keep the visual redesign conservative

The v0.8 is already structurally closer to the intended instrument than the rejected oversized versions. Improve alignment, spacing and readability only after the content map is correct.

## 15. Conclusion

The PIR should be retained. It is necessary because the framework needs a periodic layer that does not merely collect events but asks whether the site’s controls are still functioning.

The current v0.8 has the right conceptual structure and should not be replaced by a larger dashboard. Its principal defect is not lack of content; it is **loss of identity between the indicator, its rule, its denominator, its source records and its action route**.

The correct architecture is:

```text
pre-specified indicator configuration
→ defined study–site–period and population
→ exact source reconciliation
→ indicator-specific numerator/denominator or value
→ rule comparison
→ bounded interpretation
→ named action route
→ effectiveness evidence
→ period conclusion and controlled review
```

The most important correction is to ensure that every row can be followed without ambiguity from the catalogue to the final action. If that is fixed, the PIR will perform its intended role as the site-level performance-oversight layer of the Quality Lifecycle Framework, while remaining distinct from FUCR, IMC, CAPA, SRR, POT and the sponsor’s own quality systems.
