A
Purpose and use

A milestone is a point in the investigation where a failure, if it goes unnoticed, repeats in every case that follows: the first procedures, the immediate postoperative period, the approach to endpoint assessment, close-out. At each milestone the PI takes a gate decision: the unit concerned (the site, a surgeon, a process or a cohort) may take its next step, may take it only within stated limits, or must wait. Participants move through the phases at different times, so a gate decides a step for its unit, not a pause for the whole study.

The MRL has two parts. Part 1 is the plan: which milestones this investigation uses, what triggers each one and what must be shown at it, approved by the PI before the first participant, with an index of every gate decision taken. Part 2 records one gate review, in a new copy each time. The MRL cites the records that hold the evidence; it does not repeat them, and its decisions do not close them.

Decision standard
Gate decisionUse whenThe decision must state
PassThe trigger was reached for the right unit and denominator, the evidence supports the next step and known limitations do not prevent it.The scope now permitted. Pass does not mean clinical results are good.
Pass with conditionsThe evidence supports the next step only within an explicit limit.Permitted and prohibited scope; for each condition its owner, destination record, clearing evidence, re-review trigger and what happens if it is not met.
BlockedThe trigger, an essential control, an authorization or decision-critical evidence is missing, insufficient or materially uncertain.Exactly what cannot proceed and the evidence needed to re-review. A block need not stop the whole study.
