7.p. 57 · 4.3, parágrafo das três funções de risco (inteiro)
Original (marcado)
Three risk-management functions therefore sit side by side in the conformity chain. ISO 14971 governs the risk the device itself carries, and that risk stays with the manufacturer whatever site runs the investigation. ICH E6(R3) and the EMA reflection paper govern risk at trial level, through a cycle of assessment, control, communication, and review that the sponsor operates across every participating center. Between them sits a risk that neither function is positioned to see, the risk that a specific site's own execution creates. The sponsor's cycle has to generalize, because the same protocol runs at centers that differ in staffing, workflow, equipment, and experience, and a control built for one center's specific weaknesses would not travel to the next. The site does not carry that constraint, and it holds something the sponsor structurally cannot: a working knowledge of where its own process is strong and where it is not. Risk management at site level is quality-based in the same sense as the sponsor's own system, anchored in CtQ factors and a prevent, detect, and respond logic, but it operates at the one grain only the site can supply.
Reescrita
The conformity chain therefore holds three risk-management functions. ISO 14971 governs the risk carried by the device itself, which stays with the manufacturer whatever site runs the investigation. At trial level, ICH E6(R3) and the EMA reflection paper govern risk through a cycle of assessment, control, communication, and review that the sponsor runs across all participating centers. The third risk lies between these two and comes from the way a specific site executes the investigation, and neither function is placed to see it. The sponsor's cycle has to generalize, because the same protocol runs in centers with different staffing, workflow, equipment, and experience, and a control designed for the weaknesses of one center would not work in the next. 16.p. 60–61 · 4.3.3, 4.º parágrafo (de “Left to the reporting cycle…” até o fim)
Original (marcado)
Left to the reporting cycle alone, a pattern of complications becomes visible to the site itself only when the next periodic summary, monthly, quarterly, or whatever the CIP sets, is compiled for the sponsor, and by then several more cases may already share the same cause. Closing that interval is harder than tracking events as they occur, because a single complication rarely announces itself as part of a pattern. The real difficulty is aggregation, recognizing that several events, each individually explicable on its own, share a cause, which only becomes visible once they are reviewed together on a cadence the site sets for itself, independent of the sponsor's reporting calendar, so that a cluster tied to one implant component, one technique, or one surgeon can be caught while the investigation can still act on it. Reescrita

A single complication rarely looks like part of a pattern when it occurs. The pattern appears when events that each have a plausible explanation are reviewed together and turn out to share a cause, so the site reviews them on a cadence of its own, independent of the sponsor's reporting calendar. A cluster linked to one implant component or one technique, or to one surgeon, can then be caught while the investigation can still act on it. Meeting that standard starts with deciding, in advance, exactly what acquisition requires: the modalities, the time points, the conditions, the mandatory views, and, in an orthopedic investigation, matters like patient positioning, laterality confirmation, and the acquisition window at each visit. The difficulty is specificity without duplication, since the requirement has to be concrete enough to remove ambiguity at the point of acquisition while staying within what the sponsor's own imaging manual already sets, so the site is not left maintaining a second, competing standard.
Reescrita
The first difficulty is to specify acquisition in advance. The site has to define the modalities, time points, conditions, and mandatory views, and in an orthopedic investigation also patient positioning, confirmation of laterality, and the acquisition window at each visit. The specification must be concrete enough to remove ambiguity at the moment of acquisition and still stay within the sponsor's imaging manual, so that the site does not maintain a second standard that competes with the sponsor's.

31.p. 64 · 4.4.2, 6.º parágrafo (inteiro)mudança além da forma
Original (marcado)
Site qualification and personnel training determine whether radiology staff, research staff, and assessors can produce technically acceptable and protocol-compliant images. In shoulder radiography, views only five degrees from true anteversion can alter the critical shoulder angle by more than two degrees [81], and measurements deviate materially beyond approximately nine degrees of malrotation [82]. In routine practice, only 22% of images in one study met the standard anteroposterior projection [83]. Projection also produced ranges of 14 to 17 degrees in arthroplasty inclination measures [85], while standardized outlet views achieved interobserver reliability up to an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.91 for acromiohumeral distance [84]. These findings justify explicit qualification, training, and calibration of acquisition and assessment procedures.
Reescrita
The second difficulty is site qualification and personnel training, which decide whether the radiology and research staff, and the assessors, can produce technically acceptable, protocol-compliant images. In shoulder radiography, a view only 5 degrees from true anteversion can change the critical shoulder angle by more than 2 degrees [81], and measurements deviate materially beyond about 9 degrees of malrotation [82]. In one study of routine practice, only 22% of images met the standard anteroposterior projection [83]. Projection also produced ranges of 14 to 17 degrees in arthroplasty inclination measures [85], while standardized outlet views reached interobserver reliability of up to an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.91 for acromiohumeral distance [84]. These findings justify explicit qualification and training, with calibrated acquisition and assessment procedures.
Todos os números e citações foram conferidos um a um. five/two/nine degrees viraram algarismos (5, 2, 9), porque são medidas, e já havia 14 to 17 degrees em algarismos no mesmo parágrafo.
32.p. 64–65 · 4.4.2, 7.º parágrafo (até “…does not remove them.”; o resto não foi marcado)
Original (marcado)
A standardized local review process is the third difficulty, and it marks a real limit on what the site can claim for itself. Centralized image review would require trial-level coordination and cross-site governance that lies outside the control of any individual site, so it cannot be an internal operational control; only the consistency of the site's own review is within its reach. That narrower target still has to answer real questions: who reviews, against what criteria, when a second reviewer or clinical escalation is required, and how a non-conforming image is handled and documented. Leaving those questions open does not remove them. It only means they get answered differently, and perhaps inconsistently, case by case.
Reescrita
The third difficulty is a standardized local review process, and here the site reaches a limit on what it can claim. Centralized image review needs coordination at trial level and governance across sites, which no single site controls, so it cannot be one of the site's internal operational controls. What the site can control is the consistency of its own review. Even this narrower goal needs answers on who reviews and against which criteria, on when a second reviewer or a clinical escalation is required, and on how a non-conforming image is handled and documented. If the site leaves these questions open, they do not disappear. It only means they get answered differently, and perhaps inconsistently, case by case.
